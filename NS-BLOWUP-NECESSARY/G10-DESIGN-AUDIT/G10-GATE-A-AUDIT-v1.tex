\section{What theorems require - explicit checklist}
We check two canonical families (no new hypotheses):

1. \textbf{ESS backward uniqueness} for $\partial_t+\Delta$:
$| \partial_t u+\Delta u|\le C(|u|+|\nabla u|)$ in $Q=\mathbb R^3\times(-1,0]$, $|u(x,t)|\le \exp(C|x|^2)$, $u(x,0)=0$ in $\mathbb R^3$ $\Rightarrow u\equiv0$ in $Q$. For NS: applied to $\omega$ or $u$, needs differential inequality with bounded coefficients depending on $u$, and vanishing at final time.

2. \textbf{Unique continuation / Carleman}: If $u$ satisfies Stokes-type inequality and $u=0$ on open set or to infinite order at a point $(x_0,t_0)$, then $u\equiv0$. Requires vanishing of infinite order: $\forall k, |u(x,t)|\le C_k(|x-x_0|+|t-t_0|^{1/2})^k$ near $(x_0,t_0)$.

In both cases required variable: $u$ or $\omega$ or $\nabla u$.
Required region: all $\mathbb R^3$ at $t=0$ for BU, or open set / point for UC.
Required property: vanishing, smallness, decay, or bounded coefficients.
Pressure must satisfy compatible condition.
Need space-time window, not single $t_n$.

\section{What C\_PROVEN gives - point by point}
1. Variable: $C_F^{conc}$ gives $\|u(t_n)\|_{L^3(B_{r_n}(x_n))}\ge\varepsilon_0$. This is lower concentration, not upper smallness nor vanishing. Variable is $u$ in $L^3$ local, not pointwise bound.

2. Region: Balls $B_{r_n}(x_n)$ shrinking $r_n\to0$, $x_n\to x^*$. No information of $u=0$ outside. No open set where $u\equiv0$. No global decay. $C_E^{weak}$ gives $L^2$ global but not pointwise $e^{C|x|^2}$ bound nor $L^\infty$.

3. Vanishing vs lower bound: $\boxed{\text{lower concentration}\neq\text{vanishing order}}$. ESS BU requires $u(\cdot,0)=0$. We have opposite: $\|u(t_n)\|_{L^3(B_{r_n})}\ge\varepsilon_0$ and $t_n\uparrow T^*$. No $u=0$ at blow-up time, no infinite-order vanishing. $C_J^{neg}$ is loss of direction regularity, not gain of vanishing.

4. Smallness/decay: $C_{L^3}$ gives $\|u(t)\|_{L^3}\le C$? Actually not bounded; only necessary that if $T^*<\infty$ then $L^3$ must blow up? Wait Leray: $\|u(t)\|_{L^3}\to\infty$? BU needs small coefficients or bounded, but vanishing hypothesis missing. $C_I^{freq}$ gives $N_c\to\infty$, indicating high-frequency creation, opposite of smallness.
