Candidate $C_G$:

There exists $\epsilon_0>0$ (universal) and sequences
$t_n\uparrow T_*$, $x_n\in\mathbb R^3$, $r_n\downarrow 0$ such that for
$B_n:=B(x_n,r_n)$,

\begin{equation*}
\boxed{\|u(t_n)\|_{L^3(B_n)} \ge \epsilon_0}
\end{equation*}
or scale-invariant local quantity
\begin{equation*}
\boxed{r_n^{-2}\int_{B_n}|u(t_n)|^3 \ge \epsilon_0^3 \text{ or } r_n^{-1}\|u\|_{L^3(B_n)}^3 \ge \epsilon_0}
\end{equation*}
with precise scaling to be fixed, and $r_n\sim \sqrt{T_*-t_n}$ if parabolic.

Why this is non-trivial:

$\|u(t)\|_3\to\infty$ in limsup is global. Does not a priori imply mass piles at small scale; could spread. To get concentration, need profile decomposition / compactness up to scaling for Navier-Stokes.

Known results: ESS / Kenig-Koch non-concentration vs concentration: For Leray-Hopf, if $T_*<\infty$, then there exists such concentration (Escauriaza-Seregin-Sverak type). But proof uses backward uniqueness, Carleman, $\varepsilon$-regularity. We cannot assume; we must audit steps.

Steps to PROVE $C_G$ from $C_{L^3}$:

1. Rescaling: Suppose $\|u(t_n)\|_3 = M_n\to\infty$. Define $v_n(y):= r_n u(x_n+r_n y, t_n)$ with $r_n = M_n^{-?}$ to normalize $L^3$.
Scale invariance: $\|v_n\|_3 = \|u(t_n)\|_3$ global, but local norm $\|v_n\|_{L^3(B_1)}$ should carry lower bound.

2. Need non-vanishing lemma: $\limsup\|u(t_n)\|_3=\infty$ implies after suitable translation+scaling, weak limit non-zero. This is profile decomposition for $L^3$ (critical). Requires.

3. Then $\epsilon_0$ appears as minimal $L^3$ mass of non-trivial profile.

Thus $C_G$ is not immediate from $C_{L^3}$ by Holder; needs compactness.
